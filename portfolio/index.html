Consulting · Live demo

trunk'd

Public-safety radio, made legible — RF to browser in under a second.

Launch live demo →public demo · no login

Trunked P25 radio — what fire, EMS, and police run — is incomprehensible at volume: dozens of simultaneous talkgroups, no thread, no context. trunk'd captures that traffic off software-defined radios, transcribes each transmission as it lands with a local Whisper-compatible engine, and serves a live, geofenced incident board to any browser.

It's event-sourced on NATS JetStream — a durable log as the source of truth — with single-purpose Go services, each owning its own store:

Consulting · Live demo

Vega-Bray Observatory

Reverse-engineering a 20-inch telescope mount that hadn't moved in 15 years.

Launch live demo →runs in simulation

The 20-inch Maksutov at Vega-Bray had been dark for ~15 years, its original control electronics gone — no docs, no protocol, no reference hardware. I treated it as a reverse-engineering problem: reconstructed the gear ratios by photographing gear trains and counting teeth, then verified against live motion.

New ESP32 firmware (portable C++17) generates step pulses in LEDC PWM silicon so WiFi and serial can't jitter the pulse train, and speaks a reverse-engineered LX200 dialect. A Go pointing service owns the serial link — with a resync model for dropped exchanges, a JSON+SSE API, and a Stellarium listener — behind a night-safe web UI. make sim runs the whole stack against a simulated mount, no hardware needed.

Demoable now in simulation; the real hardware slews and tracks, but still needs supervision.

sdrlight

Scheduling commodity radios like compute.

What would it mean to treat a shelf of cheap USB software-defined radios the way Kubernetes treats compute — a pool of fungible, schedulable resources with well-defined claims against them? sdrlight is that idea, still mostly on paper.

The architecture allocates physical SDRs as cluster devices through Kubernetes DRA, then virtualizes upward: a workload asks for "20 MHz somewhere around 2.4 GHz" via a RadioClaim, not a specific dongle. The system decides which captures satisfy it, whether consumers can share one wideband capture, and how to move a claim between radios unnoticed. The piece I most want to get right is a zero-copy IQ path — a shared-memory pool of immutable loans — proven achievable inside an unprivileged pod.

Honest status: some behaviors work in a prototype; most of the architecture is still interface-scaffolding. This is where I'm building toward, not where it is.

Consulting · Details

Luna

A 1-meter lunar relief.

Luna is a 1-meter lunar relief sculpture I made mostly to see if I could.

The starting point was gigabytes of NASA elevation data — SLDEM-2015 lunar topography, delivered as high-bit-depth GeoTIFFs. I wrote a Python pipeline to ingest it, sample and rescale the heightfield, and emit tiled OBJ meshes sized to a printer bed. Getting the tiling and seam math right took longer than I expected; the pipeline also handles smoothing and depth-scaling so the relief reads at viewing distance without exaggerating the Moon's already-subtle topography.

From there: printed in PETG on a hardboard backing, assembled, mounted. PETG holds fine detail without the warping PLA can show on larger prints; the hardboard gave a rigid, light substrate.

The last layer is projection mapping. Using MadMapper, I drive animated lunar imagery — craters lit at oblique angles, terminator lines crawling across the surface — directly onto the physical relief, where the real contours interact with projected light in a way a flat screen can't. That part is genuinely fun to show people.
